Compiled from a full deep-dive of every file in this corpus (all 8 folders, ~70 docs, plus code/). Organized by system, not by source document — each entry keeps its file citation so you can trace back to the full reasoning. This is the working reference; treat it as a parts bin, not a spec.

## 1. Tire model & core physics (the load-bearing math)

- **Beckman's "Magical Trick Formula"** (34, Part 29) — the recommended ship-ready tire model: `F = B·Fz·α/(1+|A·α|^P)`. Fitted longitudinal params `(A,B,P) = (9.625, 31.0, 2.375)`. Within 10% of full Pacejka near peak force. Three tunable sliders per compound instead of 11–15 magic constants — cheap per-wheel-per-tick on mobile.
- **Combined-slip formula** (code/pacejka-reference) — normalize slip ratio and slip angle each by their own measured peak, combine via Pythagoras (`p = sqrt(S²+A²)`), scale each output force by its component's share: `fx=(S/p)·longPacejka(...)`, `fy=(A/p)·latPacejka(...)`. Public-domain (Unlicense). Known bug in the ply-steer term (`a[11]` used twice) — latent until camber ≠ 0, fix before adding camber.
- **Peak slip values**: longitudinal peak at slip ratio ≈8% (σ≈0.08), lateral peak at ≈4° slip angle (34, Beckman Part 21/22).
- **Closed-form 4-wheel weight transfer** (34, Part 27) — solved via the diagonal cross-ratio symmetry constraint `f1z·f3z = f2z·f4z`, generalizing 2D's `ad=bc`. Non-iterative, cheap per tick; `R̄_A` (cross-weight/wedge) is directly usable as a tuning knob (also 25-GARAGE-DESIGN Part 5).
- **Weight transfer (2D, prototype-simple)**: `Lf = dG + Bh/w`, `Lr = (1-d)G - Bh/w`. Worked example: 3200lb car, 20" CG height, 100" wheelbase, 1g braking → 640lb forward transfer.
- **Drag formula**: `Fd = ½CdAρv²`, air density 0.08 lb/ft³. Calibration reference: Cd 0.30/20ft² frontal area car needs ~650hp to hit 200mph.
- **Braking distance** scales with v² (not v); braking *time* scales linearly with v. Reference table at 1g: 60mph=120ft/2.74s, 120mph=482ft/5.48s, 150mph=754ft/6.85s.
- **RK4 over Euler, mandatorily** — Euler integration on a simple oscillator diverges 60% over 100s; RK4 stays stable. At 30fps/100mph a car moves ~5ft per step. Thermal throttling grows Δt, which grows integration error — physics itself degrades on a hot phone, not just frame rate (34, Part 28; 03-PLATFORM-NOTES).
- **Ragged-edge / instability meter formula** — two independent derivations converge: `sqrt(a_x²+a_y²)/(μg)` (traction circle fill, 0–1 normalized with a decay term) and `|a_y − v·ψ̇| > ε_threshold` (lateral accel vs. yaw-rate mismatch). Both are cheap — a multiply/subtract/abs the physics tick already has the numbers for. Decay rate should be handling-rating-dependent; add an input-*jerk* term (~0.64Hz reference oscillator frequency) so smooth inputs shed instability faster than jerky ones at the same load (11-SYSTEMS-SPEC; 20-CONCEPTS §1; code/prototype/README).
- **Beckman's "cup" stability region** — inside the cup, more slip → more grip (recoverable); outside, more slip → less grip (a mathematically precise, non-authored oversteer/spiral threshold).
- **Trail braking emerges for free** — if combined-slip tire modeling is correct, trail braking (carrying brake into the corner while winding in steering) naturally saves ~1 car length per significant corner. Don't script it as a bonus; avoid *preventing* it.
- **Polar moment of inertia as a second differentiation axis** (11-SYSTEMS-SPEC; 32-HERO-CAR §7.0) — `J=Σmr²`. Free (NHTSA has real mass-distribution data), makes early big-block generations straight-line-strong/reluctant-to-turn vs. later centralized-mass generations quick-to-rotate, without touching horsepower.
- **Confirmed tire-model bug + fix**: shipped placeholder constants (`latA=0.5, latB=10.0, latP=1.8`) implied μ≈8.9 (physically impossible); recalibrated `latB→1.35` gives μ≈1.2. Lesson: sanity-check any hand-picked tire constant against real μ range (1.0–1.7) via simulation before trusting it.
- **Validated benchmark**: switching a differential open→locked = 71% difference in corner-exit force at 0.7g, converting to a 2.32s/43% lap-time difference — the actual pass/fail bar for "does this tuning change matter."

## 2. Real drivetrain/engine data (directly usable as ScriptableObject content)

- **Ford firing-order acoustic families** (37-FORD-V8-AUDIO) — small-block/cross-plane V8s share one firing order (1-5-4-2-6-3-7-8) from 1965 through the 1996 modular engine despite completely different hardware; only the mid-2010s "Coyote" DOHC changed it. **Build 2 acoustic families, not 7** — massive audio-cost savings for a 7-generation hero car.
- **Cross-plane vs flat-plane crank** as a variant-level field, not a new engine family (40-FORCED-INDUCTION) — flat-plane revs higher/sounds exotic but has more vibration (lost cross-plane counterweight smoothing). Real trade-off, not a strict upgrade.
- **Period-correct forced induction**: real Paxton NOVI supercharger option existed 1965–72 (~7.5psi @ 5,500rpm, 30–75% hp gain, included a compressor bypass valve — ancestor of the modern BOV). No factory turbo ever existed for this lineage — turbo is aftermarket-only by design, supercharger is factory-plausible from gen 1.
- **Full 7-generation transmission dataset** (52-CONTENT-RESOLUTION-PASS-1, 47-PARTS-PRICING) — real gear ratios sourced for every era, e.g. late-80s T5 5-speed (3.35/1.99/1.34/1.00/0.67), mid-90s T45, early-2000s TR-3650, mid-2010s MT82 6-speed, 2022 TR-3160 6-speed manual / 10-speed auto / TR-9070 7-speed DCT (900Nm capacity, supercharged-only, no manual offered).
- **7-generation engine spec dataset**: 1965 baseline 164hp@5500rpm (degraded from ~195hp reference) up through 2022's three trims — 450hp base, 470hp flat-plane "Voodoo" (manual-only), 760hp supercharged "Predator" (automatic-only, real trim exclusivity preserved as a mechanical opposite pair).
- **Real generation-boundary confirmations, independently, 5 times**: 4-wheel disc brakes arrive 1993–94 matching the engine/axle boundary; Brembo enters 1996 matching the DOHC top trim; the 8.8" axle replaces the 9"/7.5" in 1986 matching the EFI transition; the Super 8.8 + Torsen diff arrives 2015 matching the engine/transmission generation change. Real automotive history clusters at the same points the fictional generations already break — strong validation for anchoring era boundaries to real production history.
- **Forced induction / crank-swap pricing** (47-PARTS-PRICING §1.2): period supercharger 20u/3 class pts, modern supercharger 55u/6pts, aftermarket turbo 35u/5pts, flat-plane crank swap 65u/7pts (final generation only).
- **Trim/transmission exclusivity as a design pattern**: flat-plane mid-tier trim never offered automatic; supercharged top trim never offered manual. Each top trim should exclude one transmission type for a *documented character reason*, not arbitrarily.

## 3. Damage, repair, and the mechanic economy

- **RVP's `VehicleDamage`** does real-time mesh deformation (optional Perlin noise, seamless panel-edge alignment, normal recalculation); damaged motors/transmissions should lose output *progressively*, not binarily (04-EXTRACTION-INVENTORY).
- **NFS High Stakes' 4-system damage model** (19 §2.3): Engine/Steering/Body/Suspension tracked separately, severity barometer affects look+performance, no mid-race repair, repair costs credits in the garage only. Direct wiring target for `VehicleDamage.Repair()`.
- **GT Auto's three decay archetypes** (24 Part 7.1) — **consumable** (oil, degrades with mileage, costs hp), **restorable** (rigidity, repairable indefinitely, but "beyond repair" forces buying a new body), **permanent** (engine neglect causes *irreversible* hp loss). The permanent tier is the bold, underused one — a car can be genuinely ruined by an inattentive owner. Tuning warning: GT7 players complained rigidity decays too fast — tune toward GT4/5's slower rate.
- **Two damage-economy models coexist, don't compete**: High Stakes' "repair bill for damage you caused" + GT Auto's "decay for time you let pass."
- **Mechanical-failure-from-abuse** (16-MANAGER-GENRE, F1 Manager 2024; 20-CONCEPTS §7) — sustained driving at the limit should eventually break something, linking the instability meter directly to the damage model. Produces honest comeback opportunities without rubber-banding.
- **Repair vs replace-only, priced as a rule, not a slider** (47-PARTS-PRICING §1.3): repairable parts price at ≈30% of fresh-replacement cost; parts like gearbox/clutch plate are explicitly *replace-only*, no repair price exists at all — encodes real mechanical truth into the pricing table itself.
- **CMS's 3-tier disassembly** (38-CMS-PARTS-TAXONOMY) — exterior (direct interact) → engine bay (bonnet is a *gate*) → full disassembly mode. Independently converges with this project's own 7-station garage design. Condition color-coded but *not sufficient alone* — some low-durability parts genuinely don't need replacement.
- **Granularity spectrum, validated at both ends**: Jalopy's 7 total components (minimal, legitimate design point, not "unfinished") vs. My Summer Car's per-bolt/per-wire granularity (extreme, niche-only, cited as a warning — but its "wrong tool size simply fails" mechanic is a cheap, diegetic gate worth borrowing without the full extreme).
- **Automation/BeamNG mesh-merge lesson**: an unmerged engine export produced ~2.5× draw calls (13,416 vs 5,392) and dropped 120fps→52fps. Rule: keep installation-sequence part meshes separate only while the vignette plays; merge into one mesh for every other context (menu, racing).
- **BeamNG/Wreckfest node-and-beam finding**: even in games built around soft-body simulation, players have a long-standing, unmet demand to cleanly remove an already-broken part. Validates *discrete named parts* as the right choice for this project on a usability basis, independent of the earlier cost-based argument.

## 4. Garage, property ladder, and the economy loop

- **Six garage functions, almost no game does all six**: Store · Modify · Repair · Display · Trade · Generate (24-GARAGE-SYSTEMS Part 1). Deliberately choosing which subset defines the game's identity.
- **Garage as place, zero travel** — resolves Underground 2's well-documented pacing failure (players hated driving to every shop) while keeping the "ownership feels real" benefit of a garage-as-place: make it the home screen, nothing to travel to.
- **Seven camera stations, not a walkable space** (25-GARAGE-DESIGN Part 2): Hero, Lift raised, Engine bay, Interior, Parts wall, Desk, Bay overview. Tap-to-focus, max 2 taps between any two stations, nothing interactive in bottom screen corners (thumb zones). Zero navmesh/collision cost.
- **The lift is the anchor object** — real workshop convention, gives underside access, and "raising it" is a satisfying physical beat costing one animation.
- **Cross-weight physics as a free garage-tuning axis**: the statically-indeterminate 4-wheel car (`ad=bc` neutral condition, deviation = "wedge") gives a real per-corner ride-height tuning knob that comes from the suspension model already existing, not authored separately.
- **Three tuning contexts, not one slider wall** (25-GARAGE-DESIGN Part 5): Garage (discrete parts, money, permanent) / Dyno (continuous calibration, free, returns a power curve) / Practice (continuous setup, costs a session, returns lap times+telemetry). "Parts cost money, geometry/calibration are free" — gives a broke player something meaningful to do.
- **Installation sequence as 4 generic vignettes, not per-part animation** (25-GARAGE-DESIGN Part 6): Engine bay (~8s), Underside (~10s), Wheels (~7s), Body/aero (~6s). Skippable-and-remembered; audio carries it; never blocks a race; the part itself is a mesh swap onto reusable hands/tools. Nobody else in the genre shows the install work — everyone shows a spinner.
- **Escalation by property tier, same 4 vignettes reskinned** (25-GARAGE-DESIGN Part 7): House (jack stands, poor light, alone) → Garage (two-post lift, work lamp) → Pro shop (four-post lift, air tools, helper) → Warehouse (multiple bays, team). Enormous perceived-progression return for 4 lighting setups and a few extra hands.
- **Property tier = named-effect table, never "+10% everything"** (16-MANAGER-GENRE, F1 Manager's facility model; 20-CONCEPTS §18): House=session recovery+small passive income; Garage=storage+repair discount; Pro shop=fabrication unlock+staff quality ceiling+part dev speed; Warehouse=storage at scale+sponsor attractiveness+inventory. Genre-breaking differentiator — "racing-game garages are almost universally inert" everywhere else.
- **GTA Online's passive-income gating matrix** (59-TRENDS-GTA) — a concrete, directly reusable table pairing property tier × reputation tier → income rate, where rushing a property purchase before reputation catches up yields *nothing*: teaches players to build supporting systems, not just buy the top unlock.
- **"Show the work" is the entire genre's gap** (24-GARAGE-SYSTEMS Part 10) — GT/Forza/NFS all show a spinner on part install; only Car Mechanic Simulator (not a racing game) shows the labor. Directly costed and specified as the installation-sequence system above.
- **CPM's engine-bay show + flipping economy** (28-CPM-CASE-STUDY) — a dedicated animation purely for *displaying* a tuned engine to other players, zero gameplay effect, because "ownership wants to be performed." Flipping = buy cheap/rough, restore, resell at a profit — literally the mechanic-protagonist's job, proven to hold 100M+ installs with *no racing attached*. Substitute for an audience when solo: a proper photo/share output from the Hero or Engine Bay station.
- **CrazyCar's economy schema** (04-EXTRACTION-INVENTORY, code/CrazyCar-MIT) — catalog table + ownership table + denormalized "currently equipped" field on the player record, repeated per system (parts/avatars/event tiers). MIT-licensed, directly copyable. Plus an asset-hot-update-manifest pattern for shipping new content without an App Store review cycle, and a mail-service pattern for delivering rewards.
- **Mad Max's late-game economy failure, named precisely**: players finish with 15–20k surplus scrap and nothing to spend it on. Car Wars' fix, directly transplantable: class brackets. If events are class-gated, a maxed car locks out of *lower* classes, forcing multiple builds and keeping money useful indefinitely for an authentic (not artificial) reason.

## 5. Race formats, AI, and rivals

- **Four formats, fully specified with numbers** (20-CONCEPTS §8-9,13; 41-RACE-FORMATS; 50-ACTION-PILLAR Part 3):
  - **Outrun**: free-roam, no track/laps/checkpoints, win by opening/holding a 300m gap.
  - **Touge duel**: 1v1 mountain road, proximity scoring, win via cumulative 10s lead within a threshold distance — road too tight for aggression to be viable, so clean driving is enforced by the format itself, not a penalty system.
  - **Knockout**: multi-car field, last place eliminated each lap/checkpoint.
  - **Pursuit/Escape**: hold within a proximity threshold for a duration (pursuit) or survive that duration against an aggression-active chaser (escape) — explicitly *not* at professional tier, since the Safety Rating gate there shuts off the aggression economy entirely.
- **Async ghosts + Autolog notification** (07-INFLUENCE-MAP; 19 §2.10; 41-RACE-FORMATS Part 2) — record position/rotation/speed at fixed interval on personal bests; non-blocking "[Rival] beat your time by [margin]" notification. Real Racing 3's proof point: this alone sustained a 13-year lifespan with zero netcode/matchmaking/server cost. Retired rivals' bests stay raceable forever — free narrative persistence.
- **Stars from objectives, not just position** (19 §2.9; 41-RACE-FORMATS Part 3) — up to 5 per-event objectives (top N, beat rival, beat PB, no contact, instability under threshold, win by margin), 3 stars drawn from up to 5, gate progression via a per-era total threshold. Shift's failure case: don't let one axis (racing-line points) outweigh another (dirty-driving penalty) or the whole rating becomes noise — test with a deliberately dirty driver.
- **Named rivals with real mechanical weaknesses, not a difficulty slider** (20-CONCEPTS §12; 45-RIVAL-DEVELOPMENT) — six archetypes, each with one genuine weakness tied to an actual system: Aero (weak below an aero-effectiveness speed threshold), Turbo (peaky, real turbo lag), Stiff (bad wet pace — first concrete use of the surface-grip system), Locked (poor period brakes), Light (no cooling/fuel margin in endurance), Constant (no weakness — the exception that proves the rule, and the physically-emergent trail-braking beneficiary).
- **AI intimidation as bounded, earned, per-rival parameters, not rubber-banding** (32-HERO-CAR §5.2-5.5) — four params defaulting to zero: `brakePointBias`, `passAttemptSuppression`, `defensivePositionError`, `launchReactionDelay`. Rises only with actual losses *to that specific rival*, never globally. Explicitly validated against Forza's own documented failure (below) and against a rejected flat "+0.5s catch-up boost" alternative.
- **Forza's Drivatar architecture, both the good and the cautionary parts** (60-FORZA-DEEP-ANALYSIS) — per-*segment* (not whole-lap) AI modeling, e.g. a 12-turn circuit split into ~27 separately-trained segments; a real documented failure where unbounded training on raw player data produced toxic (deliberately ramming) AI, fixed by clamping trained parameters before they reach the AI — directly validates the bounded-ceiling design above; and an admitted rubber-banding practice (cars performance-adjusted by gap-to-player despite "honestly" trained behavior) to explicitly avoid.
- **Rivals persist across the hero car's 60-year span with real ages** (30-NARRATIVE-DESIGN Part 3) — a rival who was 24 in 1968 is 78 in 2022; each exits in character (one stops driving and becomes a parts seller, one hands the car to someone worse, one becomes an engineer); they remember specifics, not a score (a part you sold them stays wrong for years; a dirty pass in the street tier is remembered when you meet again at the professional tier).
- **Rival production cost mitigation**: each lineage = one base model + era variants (shared topology), rivals at LOD1 except one hero-detail variant per lineage for the "walk around and read the bonnet" inspection mechanic — ~12–14 rival models total against 7 hero-car generations.
- **Rival inspection as an intelligence mechanic** — walk up to a rival's car at a meet, open the bonnet, read the spec. Reuses the Engine Bay camera station pointed at a non-player vehicle; free content, and it's literally the protagonist's actual skill turned into gameplay.
- **RVP's `FollowAI`/`VehicleWaypoint`** — per-waypoint percentage of top speed gives an authored racing-line speed profile for free, no optimizer needed; Ishaan35's randomize-which-path-each-AI-takes trick prevents a robotic field for near-zero extra cost.
- **Beckman's validated racing-line optimizer** (34 Part 17-18; code/prototype/RACING-LINE-ATTEMPT) — a two-pass forward/backward velocity-profile method (cap speed by cornering-grip and by acceleration-from-previous-point going forward, by braking-capacity going backward, take the minimum) is textbook-standard, non-copyrightable, and validated against the project's own tire model to be faster than a naive constant-speed line by 0.3–2.0s on wide corners. Known, honestly-flagged limitation: breaks down below ~165ft corner radius (path-length assumption). A trustworthy `dummy_line_time()` baseline function also validated to within 0.3% of Beckman's own published worked example.

## 6. Input, camera, and HUD

- **Tilt beats touch, with real HCI numbers** (22 §1.1-1.4) — 30% fewer fatal collisions, faster completion, and player-preferred vs. touch/button steering. But tilt-for-*orientation* is bad; tilt-for-*movement* (marble-maze mapping) is good. Nonlinear "tilt acceleration" gain is recommended — RVP's `MobileInput`/`MobileInputGet` already ships exactly this as an unused delta factor.
- **F1 Mobile's six-configuration taxonomy** (22 Part 2) maps the entire mobile control design space (tilt/buttons/virtual-wheel × auto/manual throttle-brake split) — every scheme needs a mandatory handedness mirror.
- **Driver's four-button traction vocabulary** (21 §2.1-2.3) — Accelerate/Brake/Handbrake(locks rear)/Burnout(breaks traction under power)/steering-lock-to-direction, each a genuinely different tool with a different speed cost (burnout turns faster than handbrake at low speed but handbrake scrubs less overall). Real named techniques (bootleg turn, J-turn/Rockford turn) are free narrative/tutorial content.
- **Never share an axis with steering** — Driver: San Francisco mapped boost to up-on-the-same-stick-as-steering and players triggered it constantly by accident; worse on a touchscreen with zero tactile feedback. Hard rule.
- **The garage-audition skill gate, done right** (21 §3.1-3.2) — 9 manoeuvres in 60 seconds, any order, each exercising a different input, doubling as a narrative "prove yourself" beat. Driver's own version failed because it was mandatory/unskippable/too hard; fix = instant retry, visible failure reason, partial-pass credit.
- **Assist stack reframed as "DWIM" (Do What I Mean) corrective inputs, not generic difficulty** (22 Part 4) — understeer fix = weight transfer forward via trailing throttle/light brake **and less** steering lock (opposite of instinct); oversteer splits into trailing-throttle-oversteer (add throttle+countersteer) vs power-oversteer (lift throttle+countersteer) — opposite fixes for similar-looking situations. Ordered removal (auto-brake→auto-steer→stability→traction control→ABS→racing line→rewind) tied to career tiers makes removing an assist a *taught lesson* (mentor names what it was doing) rather than a slider — simultaneously the difficulty curve, the tutorial, and the progression system.
- **Camera cost taxonomy, budget by view** (23 Part 2): chase/hood/roof need no interior model; cockpit needs a full interior; helmet needs interior + head simulation (most expensive in the genre). Recommended mobile set: chase default, hood as the performance option (best visibility, zero interior cost, what players will use for lap times regardless), one full cockpit for the hero car *only*, tinted glass + optional silhouette dash everywhere else (GT5's trick — "the absence became a styling choice"), speed-blur at screen edges (sells velocity and hides interior detail you can't afford to render, cheap in URP), a persistent rear-view strip for street tier.
- **Immersion cameras ≠ performance cameras** — Shift 2's helmet cam (apex-tracking, screen-edge blur, damage-dims-headlights) was widely abandoned by players for actual lap times because it reduced visibility. Don't build only the immersive one and expect it to serve both jobs.
- **Instability meter as a physical dash gauge, not a floating UI bar** (23 Part 5) — reads faster in the driver's actual sightline; telemetry as curves/bars only, never numeric tables (unreadable at 60fps on a phone).

## 7. Progression, narrative, and career structure

- **The mentor is a tutorial wearing a person, and the cheapest character in the genre** (29 §2.3; 30-NARRATIVE-DESIGN Part 1) — voice/text only, no animated body (one booth day = ~100 usable lines). His diagnostic voice translates raw telemetry into character meaning ("You're pushing on the exits, that front bar's too stiff") at zero extra implementation cost since the data already exists. He should be *sometimes wrong* (good instincts, old data) — the arc is: learn from him → begin to overrule him → eventually surpass him, with the game marking the first-overrule moment explicitly. No racing game has ever had a mentor a player-driver actually surpasses.
- **Inverted sabotage as the inciting incident** (30-NARRATIVE-DESIGN Part 2) — a cheap engine advertised at spec turns out underspec, discoverable *only* via earned dyno-reading competence (a knowledge gate wearing a story beat, retroactively validating every minute spent learning the dyno). The antagonist is a parts seller, not a rival driver — you beat him by knowing more than he does, in public, not by out-racing him. First-act choice with two real branches: tell the scene (reputation/trust, becomes the mechanic everyone trusts) or use the knowledge quietly (faster climb, resentment later, the game should remember).
- **Zero cutscenes, ever** (30-NARRATIVE-DESIGN Part 5) — every story beat happens in a screen the player is already using (garage, dyno, post-race telemetry, parts wall, a meet, the desk). Solves the genre's documented "inverse law" (deeper simulation = thinner story, held across the entire 50+ game corpus) by delivering narrative as ambient texture instead of an interruption — Ford v Ferrari is cited as proof the material works when told this way.
- **No racing game has ever had a "maker" protagonist** (29 §4.2) — every character who builds (Chumbucket in Mad Max) is a sidekick, never playable. Genuinely unclaimed narrative territory for a mechanic-driver premise.
- **Full 57-year act timeline with named beats** (33-ACT-STRUCTURE Part 1) — 1965-68 inheritance/restoration → 1969-86 street climb → 1987 the choice → 1988-90 hired work begins → 1991-93 bill comes due → 1994 the job nobody else takes → 1995 shop opens (act break, earned via reputation, not a tier unlock) → 1996 pro licence+Safety Rating → 1997-2021 act three → 2022 the legend.
- **Act two's "hired gun" loop fixes the genre's documented mid-game sag** (33-ACT-STRUCTURE Part 2) — the diagnosis: the sag isn't a content problem, act two just runs act one's loop with better cars. Fix: dyno *closed* on job cars (proves driving skill independent of building skill) across 5 distinct job types (Shakedown, Evaluation, Delivery, Sorting — "paid for the diagnosis, not the lap time", and sparingly-used Ringer drives).
- **Reputation as unmetered world behavior, not a UI bar** (33-ACT-STRUCTURE Part 3) — a binary 1987 branch (told/didn't tell) drives four downstream effects (jobs offered, part prices, event access, whether rivals race you clean) with no visible standing meter at all — cheaper and reads as better writing than a number going up.
- **Three-currency weekly choice** (33-ACT-STRUCTURE Part 5) — Job (money, costs a week) / Commission (money+reputation) / Own car (progress, no income), a genuine opportunity-cost tension that the warehouse property tier eventually resolves entirely (the payoff for reaching the top of the ladder is *not having to choose anymore*). Explicit anti-pattern flagged: no wrench minigames for commission work.
- **Named build recipes as trim-ladder progression** (13-MUSTANG-DOSSIER; 20-CONCEPTS §17; 48-RPG-SYSTEMS Part 1) — real-world 5-rung pattern (base → performance → factory hot → homologation special → tuner halo), each rung changing car *character* not just numbers (homologation = worse on road, better on track). 35 recipes total across 7 generations already drafted with era-correct naming and honest deviations where a real power step didn't exist (mid-70s ladder compressed; 2022 ladder irregular because only 3 real trims exist).
- **Two-axis progression gating, kept genuinely separate** (20-CONCEPTS §15; 48-RPG-SYSTEMS Part 2) — Money buys any part the catalogue *shows*; Reputation (Unknown→Known→Respected→Trusted) determines what's *shown at all*. Explicitly kept distinct from Property tier (facility capability) and Class brackets (event entry by car stats) — four separate gating axes, deliberately not collapsed into one meter.
- **Safety Rating as a bounded dual-axis system** (7-INFLUENCE-MAP, iRacing; 48-RPG-SYSTEMS Part 3) — decreases for contact/off-track cuts/causing spins (scaled by severity), increases for clean overtakes/zero-incident finishes; never penalizes instability-meter limit-driving or recovery (the key distinction — sliding at the edge is physics, not conduct). Gates professional-tier knockout entry via a locked entry list, not a punishment screen.
- **Diagnostic skill tracked as reps, not points** (49-PLAYER-CHARACTER-RPG Part 2) — a simple use-count tracker on rival inspection/telemetry/dyno; thresholds unlock more *information shown*, not new mechanics; the mentor's commentary shortens as the count rises — instrumenting his own obsolescence arc for free.
- **Emergent reputation traits from data already tracked** (49-PLAYER-CHARACTER-RPG Part 3) — "Clean racer" (high Safety Rating, raises rivals' pass-suppression toward you), "No stranger to contact" (raises their defensive error against you), "Reads a car fast" (high diagnostic-use rate, earlier tell reveal) — a full trait system built from reads of existing state, feeding back into AI behavior, no new authored content.

## 8. Aggression, stunts, and the action layer

- **Aggression made economically rational, not just permitted** (17, Burnout's Takedown; 20-CONCEPTS §10) — contact rewarded (nitrous charge, small cash), keeps physics honest since it's not weapons-based.
- **Three named aggression triggers** (50-ACTION-PILLAR Part 1.1): Forced error (contact causes a lost line, no spin), Takedown-adjacent (contact causes a spin/retire — strongest reward, highest cost), Held under pressure (maintain position while a rival tries contact on you).
- **The reward/cost balance flips by career tier, mechanically** (50-ACTION-PILLAR Part 1.2-1.4) — at street tier, reward > cost; at Contender license grade, the same contact risks the license itself. This *is* the "unlearning" arc the mentor talks about, made numeric.
- **Stunt/close-call scoring reuses the instability meter for a second job** (50-ACTION-PILLAR Part 2) — a "close call" stunt type triggers off the meter's fill level as a near-miss signal; feeds nitrous by raising the tire model's peak-longitudinal-force ceiling (not a flat speed multiplier, so it stays physically grounded). Active in Outruns/free-roam, explicitly disabled in Touge duels/Knockout — systems earn their place per format rather than being globally on.
- **Scarcity as the cheapest depth available** (18 §1.2, Top Gear's 3 non-replenishing nitro boosts per race) — a binary button made interesting entirely by making it finite.

## 9. Audio

- **Two-family engine acoustic strategy** (see §2 above) drastically cuts the reference-doc's own 52-loops-per-vehicle full spec down to a realistic ~16–20 loops/vehicle at 2 perspectives (exhaust+interior), 7–9 RPM loops each (10-AUDIO-DESIGN).
- **Loop-naming formula**: `Loop Fundamental Frequency × 60 ÷ cylinder count × 2 = exact RPM` — name files by the *computed* exact RPM, never a rounded value, or crossfades misalign.
- **No more than 2 waveforms should ever cover the same RPM range** — three muddies the sound and wastes voices; all loops must share one pitch curve (FMOD Autopitch-equivalent) to avoid detuning at crossfades.
- **Derive the Load parameter from the real drivetrain's torque output** (TORSION is already integrated) instead of approximating it — "the thing most indie games miss," and it's a near-free win given the drivetrain math already exists.
- **Off-throttle audio workaround**: skip the hard-to-record off-throttle loop set entirely; use real-time DSP (low-pass filter + volume dip on lift) plus layered overrun-pop one-shots, driven by throttle state.
- **Three-stage tire-limit audio state machine** (34 Part 2; 10-AUDIO-DESIGN) — squeak (approaching limit) → squeal (at limit) → squall (over limit, actually *lower* pitch as the slide passes optimum) — crossfaded on the exact same normalized value driving the visual instability meter and the haptic engine. A "launch hiss" at a correctly-executed launch is a real audio skill-tell (34 Part 3, Corvette worked example: too much throttle spins the tires audibly before ~320lb transfers rearward).
- **iPhone haptics architecture** (36-IPHONE-SPECIFIC) — two event types: Transient (wall hits, gear shifts, the launch hiss) and Continuous (instability meter fill, mapped 1:1 linearly as an honest starting baseline before curve-fitting). Apple's own warning: "a haptic per render is a buzzing brick" — debounce, don't retrigger every frame. Keep the continuous "engine rumble" pattern on a *separate* `CHHapticEngine` instance from transient impacts. AHAP files are declarative haptic assets, same production model as audio.
- **Record your own reference audio** — a friend's car, a handheld recorder, a quiet industrial estate, intake/exhaust/interior recorded separately — a practical, legally clean sourcing route that sidesteps the corpus's own documented mess of mixed CC0/CC-BY/CC-Sampling-Plus licenses in RVP's bundled audio.

## 10. Art, production budgets, and asset sourcing

- **Concrete triangle budget table** (09-ASSET-PRODUCTION) — scene ceiling 300k–500k tris on-screen at 60fps mid-range mobile; hero car LOD0 25,000–40,000; opponent close 15,000–20,000; opponent mid-field 5,000–8,000; shared wheel/tire library 2,000–4,000; track tile 500–2,000; prop 100–500. Minimum 3 LOD levels (100/50/20%) for anything near the frustum.
- **Texture budget**: 1K–2K mobile targets vs 4K PC; car body 2K albedo/2K normal/1K packed ORM; pack occlusion/roughness/metallic into one RGB texture (every sampler costs on tile-based mobile GPUs).
- **4–8 weeks per game-ready hero car for a senior artist** (09-ASSET-PRODUCTION; validated independently by 32-HERO-CAR's 28-56 week total for 7 generations) — the hard constraint behind "small roster, deep tuning" as a strategy, not a preference.
- **Mobile rendering rules that gate what art direction is even achievable** (08-ART-DIRECTION): URP forward-only, baked lighting + one real-time directional light max, baked cubemap reflection probes (no real-time reflections), overdraw named as the #1 thermal-throttling cause, vertex colors preferred over extra texture samplers.
- **"Sustained after 10 minutes" as the actual performance target**, not first-lap frame rate — a scene that holds 60fps for 30 seconds then throttles to 40fps for 4 minutes is a scene that must be trimmed.
- **Per-act visual arc costs nothing extra** (08-ART-DIRECTION) — street tier (sodium vapor, wet asphalt, noisy/hard-to-read) vs professional tier (clean midday sun, wide, calm) are environments you need anyway; the *readability difference* between them becomes a free difficulty curve.
- **Legal asset-sourcing map**: CC0 (Kenney, Quaternius, KayKit, Poly Haven, ambientCG) is fully safe; Sketchfab/Poly Pizza/OpenGameArt are mixed-license (check per-asset); **no good CC0 car-model source exists** — the hardest sourcing gap identified, with 4 concrete resolution paths (model in-house, commission 3-4 hero cars and build variants, buy non-exclusive Asset Store packs, or design original vehicles to sidestep trademark risk entirely).
- **Maintain an `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` from the first imported asset** — RVP's own bundled audio folder is cited as a real cautionary mess of untracked mixed CC0/CC-BY/CC-Sampling-Plus licenses.

## 11. Legal / derivation method (how to use real-world reference safely)

- **The Towle test, with real case law** (12-DERIVATION-METHOD; 13-MUSTANG-DOSSIER) — *Carroll Shelby Licensing v. Halicki* (9th Cir. 2025): a fictional vehicle becomes protectable "character" IP via (1) physical+conceptual identity, (2) consistent traits across appearances, (3) distinctive expression beyond generic/stock. "Eleanor" failed all three because her appearance varied film to film; only two specific styling elements (hood + inset-light design) were ever enforceable. Cuts both ways: stay safe by varying ≥3 defining features from any single real car; make your *own* hero car protectable later by keeping it visually consistent and narratively distinctive across every appearance.
- **The 7-step derivation pipeline** (12-DERIVATION-METHOD) — name the response in concrete physical terms → inventory 8–15 discrete features → split each into protected vs. transferable (default to protected when unsure) → trace upstream to what the reference itself was referencing (often better, unprotected real-world material) → plan substitutions → apply the distance test (≥3 changes from any single real product, no names/marks anywhere including texture filenames, second-reviewer gut check) → write a standalone build brief. Comes with a worked example (Mad Max Interceptor → concrete proportions + poly/texture budget).
- **Real film-industry precedent for exactly this practice**: Gran Turismo licensed Porsches indirectly via RUF; Ford v Ferrari used $130-300k Superformance GT40 replicas instead of $10M+ originals, with only *one* car getting a real Ford engine for the shots where the engine bay is actually visible; John Wick's "Boss 429" was secretly a Mach 1 with the target's defining visual cues (hood pins, wheels) grafted on; Fast & Furious decoupled stunt-car mechanics, dubbed engine audio, and hero-shot engines into three entirely separate sourcing pipelines. Direct precedent for "detail where the camera looks" and for treating mesh/audio/physics as genuinely separable production pipelines.
- **GTA's fictional-brand technique, fully transplantable** (15-DERIVATION-CASEBOOK) — 36 fictional manufacturers each mapped to a real brand; individual models blend ≥3 real donor vehicles (the Banshee: Viper body + Firebird scoops + Stealth headlights); naming is witty rather than imitative (Grotti←Italian "grotto," ironic Ferrari jab); a nameplate's design evolves across sequels while keeping its identity. A ready template for building 2-3 fictional brands with genuine design languages.
- **Game mechanics, systems, and UI interaction patterns are not copyrightable at all** — the only real caution is trade dress (don't clone another game's *entire* UI look-and-feel as one bundled package).
- **Track design via blended derivation, not tracing** (44-TRACK-ROSTER Part 3) — one circuit built around a single real principle (blind-crest character), one deliberately de-fingerprinted via multiple documented deviations, one blended from several real circuits generally — same method as the car-derivation pipeline, applied to track layout.
- **Real, freely-usable regulatory data**: NHRA/UEM drag distances (660ft/1,320ft/1,000ft) and oval classifications (short track <1mi up to 30° banking / intermediate 1.5mi 12-24° / superspeedway 2.5mi 28-33°) carry zero IP risk and are directly implementable.
- **Simulated banking finding worth keeping**: at 28-33° banking the grip-limited formula implies 246-327mph, but 327mph would need ~2,150hp to overcome drag — meaning superspeedway top speed is actually *power-limited*, not grip-limited. Don't present the grip-formula number as an achievable target; compute real top speed per-car from the dyno.

## 12. Code architecture and licensing (what to actually build on)

- **License map, verified per-project** (01-LICENSING): RVP = MIT code / public-domain custom art / mixed-license audio needing per-clip attribution audit. TORSION Community Edition = MIT, code-only (art excluded). CrazyCar = MIT, schema fully reusable. PERRINN Project 424 and bundled Vehicle Physics Pro = no usable commercial license, read-only reference only — and VPP's free "Community Edition" specifically caps at 1 vehicle per scene (no AI opponents possible) and desktop-only builds, a structural dead end for this project regardless of price.
- **TORSION's bidirectional torque architecture is the single most valuable pattern in the survey**: every component exposes `GetDownstreamTorque()` and `GetUpstreamAngularVelocity()` — torque flows to the wheels, angular velocity flows back to the engine. RVP's own `DriveForce` only approximates this one-way. Preserve this pattern even if replacing the surrounding code; do not flatten it when integrating with RVP.
- **RVP's exact triaged API surface** — full parameter lists already extracted for suspension/tire (camber curves, Ackermann factor, slip-dependence modes, load-sensitivity/compression-friction, RPM-bias curve), aero/drift-assist (downforce curve, auto-steer drift assist for accelerometer input), scoring (`StuntManager`/`StuntDetect`, threshold+multiplier combo system, directly reused by the action-pillar spec above), and mobile input (`MobileInput`/`MobileInputGet`'s nonlinear delta factor).
- **Unity 6 migration is fully scoped and low-risk**: 27 sites across 13 files, all mechanical renames (`Rigidbody.velocity→linearVelocity`, `.drag→linearDamping`, 3× `FindObjectOfType` calls) — no structurally-removed APIs. Tire shader porting narrows to one ~15-line vertex-deformation function, identical in both shader variants; the one real gotcha is the `ShadowCaster` pass needs the deformation reapplied explicitly (Built-in got it for free via `addshadow`, URP doesn't).
- **Consistency rule, proven by a real historical failure**: the dyno UI, the instability meter, and the car's actual driving physics must all read from the *same* tire-model instance. Underground 2 shipped with its stock differential reading as fastest "even though it doesn't make sense" specifically because the tuning UI and the physics disagreed — a named, avoidable bug class.
- **iOS-specific gotchas** (03-PLATFORM-NOTES) — IL2CPP forbids JIT; generic virtual methods crash at runtime with "no ahead-of-time code was generated," common in generic repository/event-bus patterns progression systems accumulate; managed-code stripping silently breaks reflection-based JSON serializers unless guarded with `link.xml`+`[Preserve]`. Mitigation: build Android with matching IL2CPP+ARM64+stripping settings to catch most of this class on a physically-reachable device before it ever reaches an Xcode-only crash.
- **Device-tier QA discipline, stated as a hard rule everywhere it's relevant**: any tuning/handling change must be verified on both a Pro-tier (120Hz) and base-tier (60Hz) device, and after ~10 minutes of thermal soak, because the same code can be perceptible on one tier and imperceptible on the other.
