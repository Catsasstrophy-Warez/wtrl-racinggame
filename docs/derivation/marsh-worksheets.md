# Derivation Worksheet: Marsh — 1965

Reference:        Lightweight rear-drive economy-compact class, first generation
Domain:           Vehicle model
Date:             production-pending
Target in game:   Marsh, era 1 of 7 (`30` §3.3)

---

## 1. What is the response?
A small, upright, unadorned two-box or three-box compact — the class's
whole identity is modesty. Small round headlamps, thin chrome trim
(minimal, not absent — full trim-delete wasn't yet a styling language in
this period), narrow tyres, and a proportion that reads as economical
rather than sporting, deliberately unlike every other car in the roster.

## 2. Feature inventory
1. Small, upright two- or three-box proportion
2. Small round headlamps
3. Thin chrome trim, minimal but present (period-correct)
4. Narrow tyres on small-diameter steel wheels
5. Simple, flat grille with minimal ornamentation
6. Single round taillamp per side
7. Plain, unadorned door panels — no character lines
8. Small, simple bumpers, minimal chrome

| 6 | Single round taillamp | No | Universal period convention |

| 8 | Simple bumpers | No | Universal period convention |

**Protected count:** 0    **Transferable count:** 8

Traces to the broader real economy-compact category of this period —
several manufacturers built comparable modest compacts simultaneously,
responding to shared market pressure, not one firm's specific design.

The real category's genuine birth in this window: a small, practical
hatchback body given a modestly larger engine and slightly wider tyres,
but — critically, matching Marsh's stated philosophy (`30` §3.2) —
**deliberately not visually shouting about it.** No large spoilers, no
aggressive graphics; the class's earliest examples were notable
specifically for looking almost identical to their non-performance
siblings.
