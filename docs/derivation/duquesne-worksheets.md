# Derivation Worksheet: Duquesne — 1965

Reference:        American big-block muscle class, first generation
Domain:           Vehicle model
Date:             production-pending
Target in game:   Duquesne, era 1 of 3 (`30` §3.3)

---

## 1. What is the response?
A long-hood, short-deck two-door coupe silhouette — the defining
proportion of the entire American muscle-car category at its origin.
Twin round or stacked headlamps, a wide chrome grille, and a hood scoop
or bulge hinting at the big-block underneath.

## 2. Feature inventory
1. Long-hood, short-deck proportion
2. Twin round or stacked headlamp arrangement
3. Wide chrome grille, horizontal bars
4. Functional or decorative hood scoop/bulge
5. Coke-bottle-adjacent hip line (a common period silhouette convention)
6. Chrome bumper, full-width front and rear
7. Redline or whitewall-adjacent tyre styling, period-correct
8. Dual exhaust exiting under the rear bumper

Traces to the broader early-60s American performance-coupe category's
shared response to available big-block displacement — a genuine
industry-wide moment (several real manufacturers launched comparable
cars within a few years of each other), not one firm's proprietary
concept.

# Derivation Worksheet: Duquesne — 1980s

Reference:        American big-block muscle class, emissions-era decline
Domain:           Vehicle model
Date:             production-pending
Target in game:   Duquesne, era 3 of 3

---

## 1. What is the response?
The real class's genuine decline period — smaller, emissions-strangled
engines under the same visual bravado, a more restrained hood scoop
(often non-functional by this point), and the beginning of aerodynamic
concerns showing in a shallower front air dam. **This is the era `30`
§3.3 has Duquesne exit the lineage** — proper differentials and
wet-weather tyres from elsewhere make his brute-force philosophy
obsolete, and this generation should visually foreshadow that ending.

## 2. Feature inventory
1. Smaller, often non-functional hood scoop
2. Shallower front air dam (early aero-consciousness)
3. Narrower chrome trim (cost and weight-reduction era)
4. Digital or semi-digital dash cluster (period-correct)
5. Slightly smaller wheel/tyre combination than the 70s peak
6. Simplified taillight graphic, fewer segments
7. Single, smaller exhaust tip (emissions-era restriction)
8. Less pronounced rear arch flare

## 3. The split

| # | Feature | Protected? | Why |
|---|---------|-----------|-----|
| 1 | Smaller hood scoop | No | Functional decline, industry-wide emissions response |
| 2 | Shallower air dam | No | Functional, early aero-consciousness across the class |
| 3 | Narrower chrome trim | No | Industry-wide cost/weight era |
| 4 | Digital dash | No | Industry-wide instrumentation-technology generation |
| 5 | Smaller wheels | No | Industry-wide fitment retreat, not one firm's choice |
| 6 | Simplified taillight | No | Cost-era styling simplification, universal |
| 7 | Smaller exhaust tip | No | Functional, emissions-driven, industry-wide |
| 8 | Less arch flare | No | Reflects smaller-tyre fitment, functional |

**Protected count:** 0    **Transferable count:** 8

## 4. Upstream trace
The real class's genuine emissions-era decline, an industry-wide
regulatory response — the exact real-world pressure `30` §3.3 cites as
the reason this rival's philosophy becomes obsolete in-fiction.

Reference board: the class broadly, decline-era generation.
