# Wasteland Corpus: The Extended Lineage

Completing the Mad Max / Road Warrior / Outlander thread from `05` and `06`.

**Fit warning, stated once and then dropped.** This is a combat genre; your game
is a motorsport career sim. The extraction rate here is low compared with `07`
and `13`. It is included for completeness and for the handful of ideas that do
transfer. If you are short on time, read §4 and skip the rest.

---

# PART 1 — The Ozsploitation wave

Mad Max (1979) and more so *The Road Warrior* (1981) proved intensely popular
enough that a wave of Australian films followed in the same vein — frenetic
chases, post-apocalyptic desert, fuel scarcity. Whether Miller created the
subgenre or caught a zeitgeist is genuinely debated.

**Films in the wave:**
- **Dead End Drive-In** (1986, Brian Trenchard-Smith) — frequently cited as the
  best non-Max entry. Teenagers trapped in a drive-in turned internment camp.
- **Battletruck** (1982, aka *Warlords of the 21st Century*) — New Zealand,
  fuel-scarcity plot, an armoured truck as the antagonist
- **The Blood of Heroes** (1989) — post-apocalyptic sport rather than driving
- **Warrior of the Lost World** (1983) — low-budget, a talking motorcycle
- **Steel Dawn** (1987) — desert wanderer, water scarcity
- **The Rover** (2014) — modern Australian, minimalist, a stolen car as the
  entire plot engine

**What transfers:** almost nothing mechanically. The value is in seeing how
consistently the genre returns to **fuel and water as the plot engine** — the
same scarcity structure identified in Outlander (`05` §2.1) and the same one
that maps onto endurance fuel strategy in your professional tier.

# PART 2 — The Death Race lineage (racing as blood sport)

More relevant than the Ozsploitation wave, because these are *races*.

- **Death Race 2000** (1975, Roger Corman production) — a transcontinental race
  where points are scored for running down pedestrians. Satire.
- **Death Race** (2008, Paul W.S. Anderson, Jason Statham) — reframed as a prison
  race where inmates must brutalise and kill one another on the road to victory
- **Death Race 2** (Roel Reiné) — prequel, origins of the "Frankenstein" driver

**What transfers:**

**The persona/mask structure.** The Frankenstein driver is a *role* passed
between people rather than a person — the promoter maintains the character while
drivers die and are replaced. That is a genuinely reusable idea for a career
game: a legendary racing identity the player can eventually inherit, with all
the reputation and expectation attached.

**Scored aggression as a sanctioned mechanic.** Death Race makes hostile driving
an explicit scoring system rather than an emergent behaviour. If you build the
street-tier aggression layer discussed in `06`, an *explicit* score for it —
rather than just tolerated contact — is the cleaner design. It also sets up the
professional tier's Safety Rating (`07` §2.3) as a real reversal.

# PART 3 — Adjacent post-apocalyptic road films

Included for completeness. Most contribute atmosphere, not mechanics.

- **Damnation Alley** (1977) — the Landmaster, an articulated twelve-wheeled
  vehicle built for the film. Notable as vehicle *design* reference: a
  purpose-built machine as a character.
- **Waterworld** (1995) — scarcity economy where a resource (dry land, soil) is
  currency. The trimaran is another purpose-built hero vehicle.
- **The Book of Eli** (2010) — walking, not driving, but the cleanest example of
  the "one object everyone wants" plot engine
- **Snowpiercer** (2013) — class stratification as physical progression through
  space. A genuinely interesting structure for a career mode: each tier is a
  place you have to earn entry to.
- **The Road** (2009) — the bleakest end of the register
- **Fist of the North Star** — the anime end of the wave, wasteland martial arts
- **Escape from New York / L.A.** — Carpenter's contribution, urban rather than
  desert

# PART 4 — What actually transfers (read this section)

Ranked. Everything above is context; this is the extraction.

**1. Scarcity as the plot engine → fuel and tyre strategy.**
Every film in this lineage runs on a resource clock. Outlander (`05` §2.1) made
it a literal gauge. In your professional tier this becomes fuel load affecting
weight affecting lap time, and tyre wear affecting grip — real motorsport, and
underused on mobile.

**2. Death Race's explicit aggression scoring → your street tier.**
Make hostile driving a scored mechanic, not tolerated behaviour. Then remove it
at the professional tier via Safety Rating. The reversal is your progression arc.

**3. The inherited racing persona → career identity.**
A legendary name the player earns rather than a character they are. Pairs well
with the named build recipes in `05` §1.3 and the trim ladder in `13` §2.

**4. The purpose-built hero vehicle (Landmaster, Waterworld trimaran).**
Not a modified production car but a machine designed for the world. If your
warehouse/pro-shop tier ever unlocks a fully bespoke build, this is the
reference for treating that car as a character in its own right.

**5. Snowpiercer's spatial tiering → career structure.**
Each tier as a *place* you earn access to, rather than a menu unlock. Your
property ladder already has this shape — the paddock you can't get into yet is
more motivating than a greyed-out button.

## What to skip

Wasteland art direction (fights your premise — `05` Part 3), weapons, arena
combat, and anything requiring bespoke set-piece authoring. All expensive, none
serve a mechanic-to-professional-driver arc.
