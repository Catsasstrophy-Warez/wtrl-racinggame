# Competitor Anatomy: GRID Autosport and Car Parking Multiplayer

**Two games matter more than the other forty-eight.** One occupies the quadrant
you are aiming at. The other is winning the audience you actually want.

**This document also corrects the positioning map in `26` Part 5.** The two-axis
version understates the gap. The three-axis version is materially more useful.

---

# PART 1 — The correction

`26` mapped the field on **physics depth** against **progression depth**, and
placed GRID Autosport alone in the deep/deep quadrant. True, but imprecise — and
the imprecision hides the opportunity.

**GRID has career depth without ownership depth.**

You sign with teams. You take sponsor objectives. You have a teammate. You
progress through disciplines toward a championship. A genuine, well-built career.

**But there is no garage.** You do not buy a car. You do not own one. You do not
modify one. No parts economy, no visual customisation, no build. Setup exists;
*building* does not.

## The honest map has three axes

| | Physics | Career | Ownership |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GRID Autosport** | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Car Parking Multiplayer** | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |
| **CarX Street** | ~ | ~ | ✅ |
| **Assoluto Racing** | ✅ | ❌ | ~ |
| **CSR2** | ❌ | ~ | ✅ |
| **Real Racing 3** | ~ | ~ | ❌ |
| **You** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

> **Nobody has all three. Not one title in fifty.**
>
> And critically: **each leader is missing something structural rather than
> something they simply have not got round to.** GRID *cannot* add a garage — it
> is a frozen port of a game built around signing with teams. CPM *cannot* add
> real physics — it would break eight years of muscle memory for a hundred
> million players.

# PART 2 — GRID Autosport

## 2.1 The facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Released** | iOS 27 Nov 2017 (console original 2014). Android 2019. Also Switch. |
| **Publisher** | Feral Interactive, porting Codemasters |
| **Price** | **$9.99 one-time. All DLC free. No IAP.** |
| **Content** | 100+ cars, 100 circuits and routes, 28 circuits across 22 locations |
| **Install** | **4GB download → ~6GB installed. 8GB free space recommended.** |
| **Devices** | iPhone 6S and later, iPad 5th gen (2017) and later, any iPad Pro |
| **Controls** | Touch, tilt, MFi gamepad. Rookie→Master presets or Custom assists (TC, ABS) |
| **Disciplines** | Touring · Endurance · Open Wheel · Tuner (drift + time attack) · Street |
| **Extras** | Cross-play multiplayer beta between iOS and Android |

## 2.2 Seven exploitable weaknesses

**1. The 6GB install is a conversion catastrophe.** Feral had to split it — 4GB
from the App Store, several more gigabytes delivered as free in-app downloads,
purely to reduce the initial download. **That is a workaround for a wound.**
Every gigabyte costs installs.

**2. $9.99 with no trial** at launch. The free Custom Edition sampler came later
precisely because that wall was too high.

**3. Device requirements exclude the growth markets.** Racing revenue
concentrates in Asia, Latin America and Africa (`26` §1.1), and GRID does not run
on most hardware there.

**4. Content frozen at 2014.** A hundred cars and twenty-eight circuits, forever.
No new content has shipped and none will.

**5. Feral publicly admitted the input problem.** Their own App Store response:

> *"Although the handling model is more slanted towards simulation than other
> racing games on the App Store, we are open to refining the control systems in
> the game."*

A porting studio conceding that console handling does not map cleanly to a
phone — the exact problem `22` exists to solve. **They were porting a
wheel-and-gamepad handling model. You are not.**

**6. It is a port, not a product.** No seasons, no events, no live operation, no
community. It shipped and stopped.

**7. No ownership.** The one above all others.

## 2.3 What beating a 2014 port actually requires

Less than it sounds:

- **Ship under 2GB.** Your small roster (`13` §1) makes this achievable and it
  is a genuine competitive weapon.
- **Run on older hardware.** Rally One does this deliberately and it opens entire
  markets (`26` Part 4).
- **Have a garage.** GRID has no answer here at all.
- **Design the input natively** (`22`).
- **Keep shipping.** A live product beats a frozen one on a long enough timeline.

> You do not need to beat GRID on physics or content volume. **You need to beat
> it on install size, device reach, and the thing it entirely lacks.**

# PART 3 — Car Parking Multiplayer

Full treatment in `28-CPM-CASE-STUDY.md`. The essentials:

## 3.1 The scale

| | |
|---|---|
| **Developer** | **Olzhass — Kazakhstan.** A small studio. |
| **Released** | 20 March 2017 |
| **Portfolio** | **5 apps, ~200 million total installs, ~3 million ratings** |
| **CPM installs** | **100 million+ on Google Play alone** |
| **Active users** | **~1.7 million averaged in Europe alone** (Android, Q1 2025) |
| **Reach** | Top 100 in over ten countries |
| **India, one month** | ~2 million downloads, ~$200k revenue |

**Five apps. Two hundred million installs. A small team in Kazakhstan.**

> That is the production-model finding, and it is the one to take most seriously.
> This is not a studio that outspent anyone. It is a small team that found an
> underserved desire and served it for eight years.

## 3.2 Five limits — where you would win

**1. The physics are parking-game physics.** CPM2 improved them. They are not a
tyre model. Slip curves, load sensitivity, differential behaviour, the
instability meter — all beyond what this engine does.

**2. There is no career.** No arc, no tiers, no progression narrative. You
arrive, you build, you show off. Compelling, and it has no middle and no end.

**3. Monetisation is the top complaint.** Intrusive ads interrupting driving; the
most desirable cars behind expensive purchases. Every review names it.

**4. Performance problems on older devices.** Lag and crashes — self-inflicted in
a category defined by reach.

**5. It requires other people.** Single-player is 82 parking challenges. **Remove
the multiplayer and there is very little game.**

# PART 4 — The strategy that follows

## 4.1 Four things to take

**1. Ship small.** GRID's 6GB is its worst weakness and you can be a fifth of
that. Make it a marketing line.

**2. Build the ownership loop, and do not wait for multiplayer to justify it.**
Restoration and resale — buy rough, fix, sell for what the work is worth — is
your protagonist's literal job, and it works single-player. **CPM proves the
desire; you do not need its social layer to serve it.**

**3. Give the car somewhere to be seen.** Absent multiplayer, the substitute for
an audience is a **camera** — a framed photo output from the Hero or Engine Bay
station (`25` §7b). Cheap, feeds the same impulse, and it is free marketing.

**4. Pick a community before you pick a market.** Olzhass grew through Turkish
car culture, localised servers and regional creators (`28` Part 5). **It is the
only distribution model available to a small team, and it demonstrably works at
scale.**

## 4.2 The one-line positioning

> **GRID Autosport is a career with no garage. Car Parking Multiplayer is a
> garage with no career and no physics.**
>
> You are building the one with all three — and the fact that the two market
> leaders are each missing something they **structurally cannot add** is the
> strongest argument in this research package.
