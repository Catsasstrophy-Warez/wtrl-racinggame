# Act Structure Specification

**The fourth specification document.** Written to fix a specific diagnosis: the
1987–96 stretch was the thinnest part of the career — the club tier, where the
player is neither a street racer nor a professional, and where most racing
careers sag.

**The diagnosis was wrong in one detail, and that turned out to matter.** The sag
was never a content problem. **It was that act two had no loop of its own** — it
was act one with better cars.

Each act now has a structurally different weekly loop:

| Act | The loop |
|---|---|
| **One** | You build, you race, you climb |
| **Two** | **You drive other people's cars while your own sits waiting** |
| **Three** | **Everything competes, and the property ladder is how you win the week back** |

# PART 2 — Act two: the hired gun

## 2.1 The premise

**You are good, and nobody will give you a seat.**

So you drive other people's cars for money. Nine years of it.

**The license this whole act is proving out is now a real system**
(`49-PLAYER-CHARACTER-RPG.md` Part 1) — Contender grade specifically
requires proof across the different job types below, not repetition of
one, which is why the five-job structure exists rather than a single
repeatable task.

This is the shop-driver tier from `20` §20, expanded from a mechanic into the
entire middle act — and it is the only bridge design that solves the problem
ProStreet demonstrated (`19` §2.8): **the street audience does not follow you to
a racetrack on its own.**

> Nobody hands a street racer a professional drive. **They hand him a test
> session first.** The transition stops being a genre change and becomes a
> career step.

## 2.2 The rule that makes it work

> **You cannot tune the job car. The dyno is closed.**

Every job car is built and tuned by someone else, usually badly, and you drive it
as handed.

**This is the only way the game can prove you have driving skill separate from
building skill** — and that proof is what makes the professional tier plausible
later. Act one established that you can build. Act two has to establish that you
can drive.

It also does something quieter: **it makes the player miss their own car.** Nine
years of driving compromised machinery is nine years of wanting to get back to
the thing under the dust sheet.

## 2.3 The five job types

| Job | What it tests | Pays in |
|---|---|---|
| **Shakedown** | Drive a fresh build, report faults | Money + the owner's opinion of you |
| **Evaluation** | Set a benchmark on a car you did not tune | Money + telemetry you keep |
| **Delivery** | Move a valuable car intact, under time | Money + a contact |
| **Sorting** | Fix a car that is fast but undrivable | Money + **reputation** |
| **Ringer** | Race someone else's car under their name | Money, **no credit** |

### The sorting job is the important one

Someone hands you a car that is fast and horrible. You drive it, read the
telemetry (`20` §5), and tell them what is wrong with it.

> **You are paid for the diagnosis, not the lap time.**
>
> That is the protagonist's actual profession, monetised — and it is the job
> type that builds the reputation the shop is later made of.

**Design note:** the sorting job is where the mentor's diagnostic voice
(`30` §1.3) earns its keep at scale. He is in your ear on someone else's car,
and you are learning to disagree with him.

### The ringer job is the sharp end

**You win races nobody knows you won.** Paid, uncredited, and the result goes on
someone else's record.

Use these sparingly — three or four across the decade — and place them where the
humiliation lands hardest: the race you would have won anyway, in a car worse
than yours, for a driver who takes the trophy.

## 2.4 The tension

**You are better than the cars you are given and better than the people who own
them. Every job is a small humiliation with a payment attached.**

That is the emotional register of the whole decade, and it is what makes 1995
land.

# PART 3 — The bill: standing as a live variable

The 1987 decision (`30` §2.5) is not a delayed fuse. **It shapes every job offer
for nine years.**

## 3.1 Branch A — you told them

**You are trusted. And you made an enemy with money.**

He cannot out-drive you, so he does everything else:

- **Buys the rival you were about to beat** — that seat is gone
- **Gets you excluded from events he sponsors**
- **Makes specific parts expensive or unavailable** through his suppliers
- **Sponsors a young driver purely to beat you**

> **Trust buys you commissions and clean racing. It costs you access.**

## 3.2 Branch B — you stayed quiet

**You climbed on other people's broken engines.**

Then, around 1993, **somebody else finds out** — and the question is whether you
admit you knew.

The people who were running those engines were beating themselves for years.
Some of them are still in the paddock. Some of them are rivals you beat.

> **Silence buys you a faster climb. It costs you the room.**

## 3.3 Standing is never a number on screen

**Four visible effects, no meter:**

| Effect | How the player experiences it |
|---|---|
| **Which jobs appear** | The board is thinner or richer. No explanation given. |
| **What parts cost** | Seller by seller. The same coilover, two prices. |
| **Which events accept your entry** | Some entries are simply declined |
| **Which rivals race you clean** | Contact from specific people, specific reasons |

> **A standing bar would ruin it.** The player should infer their reputation from
> how the world behaves, which is both better writing and cheaper UI.

## 3.4 Why this bridges to the professional tier

**The professional tier is a licensing regime, and licences are granted by
people.**

> **You do not earn the pro tier by being fast. You earn it by being someone
> they will let in.**

That reframing is what makes 1996 feel earned rather than unlocked — and it gives
the Safety Rating (`20` §11) a social meaning before it has a mechanical one — the mechanical version is in `48-RPG-SYSTEMS-SPEC.md` Part 3, and this act's five job types are the reputation grind that Part 2 of that same document ties into `47`'s parts tiers directly.

# PART 4 — The act break: 1995

## 4.1 The moment

**Somebody walks in and asks you to build something.**

Nine years of driving other people's cars, and the reward is not a drive. **It is
a customer.**

## 4.2 Why the shop must be a reward, not a chapter

This is the single most important structural decision in the act.

- If the shop is a **phase you pass through**, it is a tier unlock
- If the shop is a **menu option**, it is a system
- **If the shop is the payoff for a decade, it is an act break**

The shop opens **because the reputation earned it** — specifically, because of
the sorting jobs, where you were paid for knowing what was wrong.

## 4.3 What it makes of act three

It also makes 2004's pro shop **the second half of a promise** rather than a
fresh system (`25` §4.6, `16` §4.1). The player has wanted this since 1995.

# PART 5 — Act three: the three-currency week

**The retrofit.** Combination C's depth, arriving once there is somewhere for the
tension to go.

## 5.1 The weekly choice

| Activity | Pays in | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Take a job** | Money, quickly | A week not building |
| **Take a commission** | Money **+ reputation** | A week not building — and the best briefs take longest |
| **Work on your own car** | **Progress on the hero car** | No income |

> **Three currencies — money, reputation, and the hero car — and you cannot
> serve all three.**

## 5.2 Why it only works in act three

In act two there is no shop, so there is no commission lane. In act three you
have all three **and** the pro shop and warehouse to spend on — which means the
choice has consequences that **compound** rather than merely costing a week.

**The tension needs somewhere to escalate, or it is just a menu.**

## 5.3 The pressure valve that makes it bite

> **The hero car has to be visibly starving.**

If working on your own car is optional, players will optimise into money and
never feel the choice.

**It must be losing ground:**
- A class bracket it is about to be locked out of (`06` §2.2)
- A rival whose build has moved past yours — and you can see it, because you can
  read builds (`30` §3.1)
- An event you cannot enter in current spec
- A part you won in 1993 that is now the weak link

**And the cheapest expression of all of it:** the car sitting untouched **under a
dust sheet in the corner of the bay.** Visible from the Hero station every time
the player opens the app.

> **That is the whole mechanic, and it costs one prop.**

## 5.4 The commission structure

Each commission is **a constraint you did not choose:**

| Brief | What it tests |
|---|---|
| *"Fast, but £2,000 total."* | Budget engineering |
| *"My wife has to be able to drive it."* | Driveability over pace |
| *"It has to beat the turbo man's car."* | You have read his build — you know the target |
| *"Keep it period-correct."* | No modern parts |
| *"I don't care what it costs."* | The rare one. The reputation payoff |

### Satisfaction, not a score

A commission succeeds when the car **meets the brief** — which sometimes means
deliberately building something slower than you could.

> **Building a worse car on purpose is a skill this genre has never asked for**,
> and it demonstrates engineering understanding far better than maxing does.

### The trap to avoid

**Commissions must be brief-driven, not simulated labour.** You spec it, you
shake it down, you hand it over.

**No wrench minigames.** Outlander, DRIV3R and Underground 2 all failed at
exactly this point (`21` Part 5, `19` §2.5), and this is where the temptation is
strongest.

## 5.5 The property ladder as escalation

This is what the ladder is *for*, and it finally has an emotional function
rather than a storage number:

| Tier | What it does to the three-way squeeze |
|---|---|
| **Garage** | Nothing. You choose one thing per week. |
| **Pro shop** | **Staff.** You can run a job *and* a build in the same week. **The first relief.** |
| **Warehouse** | **Multiple bays, a team.** You can run all three. **The tension resolves.** |

> **You are buying your way out of having to choose.**
>
> That is a far better reason to want a warehouse than storage capacity, and it
> is the same money the storage was going to cost anyway.

**Decided: the mechanism is labour, specifically** (`25` §6.2b). Staff let
money substitute for the "own car" slot in the weekly choice above — the
player pays, the installation happens without the mentor present (`30` §1.3),
and the freed week goes to a job or commission instead. Before the pro shop
exists, there is no substitute: every installation is the player's, and every
one is a lesson.

# PART 6 — What this fixes, restated

**The sag was not a content problem.** It was that 1987–96 had no loop of its
own — it was act one with better cars.

Now:

- **Act one** is build → race → climb
- **Act two** is drive other people's cars while yours waits
- **Act three** is everything competing, with the ladder as the way out

And **the 1987 decision stops being a delayed fuse.** It is the thing shaping
every job offer for nine years, and the thing that determines who walks through
the door in 1995.

# PART 7 — Production notes

## 7.1 Cost by component

| Component | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Job board + five job types** | **Low** | Events already specced. Job cars are rival cars reused (`30` §3.6) |
| **Standing effects** | **Low–medium** | Systemic. Four modifiers, no new UI |
| **Both 1987 branches** | **Medium** | Writing, not systems |
| **Commission generator** | **Medium** | Constraint types, satisfaction model, customer characters |
| **Three-currency week** | **Low** | It is a scheduling constraint, not a new system |
| **The dust sheet** | **One prop** | The highest ratio in the design |

## 7.2 Staging

**Act two ships before act three.** Combination B is playable without the
commission system — jobs plus standing is a complete decade.

**C retrofits cleanly** once the shop exists, which means the commission
generator can be a post-launch addition if the schedule demands it.

## 7.3 The dust sheet is not a joke

Of every item in this document, **the untouched car under a sheet, visible from
the Hero station, is the one doing the most work per unit of cost.** It converts
an abstract opportunity cost into something the player looks at every time they
open the app.

Build it early. Test whether it makes people feel guilty.

## Addendum: race formats and star-gated progression

`41-RACE-FORMATS-GHOSTS-OBJECTIVES.md` fills in what this document
assumed but never specified: **which race format belongs to which tier**
(outruns at street, touge duels through club into professional, knockout
events at professional) and how a player advances through an era's
content without winning every event outright — a star total per era
gates the next career beat, independent of individual event results,
giving a clean-but-not-always-fastest driver a real path through the
same content a race-winner takes.
